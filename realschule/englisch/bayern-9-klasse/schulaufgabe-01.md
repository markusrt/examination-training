## Part 1 – Reading Comprehension (15 BE)

Read the following text carefully. Then answer the questions below in **complete English sentences**. Use your **own words** as far as possible – do not just copy whole sentences from the text.

---

**A work placement in Manchester**

| | |
|---:|:---|
|  1 | Lisa is 15 years old and goes to a *Wirtschaftsschule* in Munich. Like all students in Year 9, |
|  2 | she has to do a two-week work placement at a real company. Most of her classmates apply |
|  3 | to local shops, banks or offices, but Lisa has a different plan: she wants to do her |
|  4 | placement abroad to improve her English. |
|  5 | After weeks of searching, she finds a small import-export company in Manchester that |
|  6 | works with German customers. The manager, Mrs Bennett, replies to her email and offers |
|  7 | her a place for two weeks in October. Lisa is excited but also nervous. She has never |
|  8 | travelled to England on her own before. |
|  9 | On her first day, Mrs Bennett shows her around the office and explains her tasks: |
| 10 | Lisa has to answer simple emails from German customers, file invoices and help in the |
| 11 | warehouse when new goods arrive. At lunch, she eats with the other employees in a |
| 12 | small canteen. Everybody speaks English, of course, and at first Lisa understands only |
| 13 | half of what they say. By the end of the first week, however, things get easier. |
| 14 | She learns a lot of new business words like *invoice*, *delivery* or *order form*. |
| 15 | When Lisa flies back home, she is tired but happy. In her report for school she |
| 16 | writes: "Two weeks ago, I was afraid of speaking English. Now I know I can do it. |
| 17 | If you ever get the chance to work abroad, take it!" Her teacher gives her the |
| 18 | best mark of the class. |

---

**a)** Where does Lisa go to school and what is special about her work placement compared to that of her classmates? [3 BE]

**b)** Why did Lisa choose a company in Manchester and not in Munich? [3 BE]

**c)** Name **three** different tasks Lisa has to do during her placement. [3 BE]

**d)** How does Lisa feel at the beginning of the placement, and how does she feel at the end? Explain the change in your own words. [4 BE]

**e)** Decide whether the following statements are **true** or **false**. Correct the false ones with a short sentence (in English). [2 BE]

  - (1) Most of Lisa's classmates also do their placement abroad.
  - (2) Lisa works alone in the warehouse the whole day.

## Part 2 – Reported Speech (10 BE)

Mrs Bennett, the manager, talks to a colleague about Lisa. Rewrite each sentence in **reported speech**. Pay attention to backshift of tenses, pronouns and time/place expressions.

**a)** Lisa said: "I am really enjoying my placement here." (Lisa told Mrs Bennett ...) [2 BE]

**b)** Mrs Bennett said: "We received a new order from Berlin yesterday." (Mrs Bennett explained ...) [2 BE]

**c)** Lisa asked: "When will I get my next task?" (Lisa wanted to know ...) [2 BE]

**d)** A colleague asked Lisa: "Have you ever worked in an office before?" (The colleague asked Lisa ...) [2 BE]

**e)** Mrs Bennett said to Lisa: "Don't forget to send the invoices today!" (Mrs Bennett reminded Lisa ...) [2 BE]

## Part 3 – Text Production: Inquiry (20 BE)

Imagine **you** are a student at a *Wirtschaftsschule* in Bavaria. Like Lisa, you are looking for a two-week work placement in **Brighton (England)** in **October**. You found the following advertisement on the internet:

> **Greenway Trading Ltd., Brighton**
> *International trading company with German business partners.*
> We sometimes offer short work placements for students from abroad.
> Contact: Mr James Carter, hr@greenway-trading.co.uk

Write a **formal email of inquiry** to Mr Carter (about **120–150 words**). Include the following points:

- a suitable greeting and a short introduction (who you are, your school, your age)
- the reason why you are writing (work placement in October, two weeks)
- ask **at least three concrete questions** about the placement (e.g. tasks, working hours, accommodation, costs, language requirements)
- mention what you can already offer (school subjects, computer skills, level of English, …)
- ask for a reply and finish with a polite closing formula

> 💡 **Remember:** Use **formal language** (no contractions like *I'm* or *don't*), correct email layout (greeting, paragraphs, closing) and **polite question forms** ("Could you please tell me …", "I would like to know whether …").
